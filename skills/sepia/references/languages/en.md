# English language layer

This layer edits for natural English while preserving the writer's variety, register, and voice. It is not a list of forbidden “AI words.”

## 1. Lock the variety and relationship

Identify, when evidence supports it:

- en-US, en-GB, en-AU, en-CA, or another established variety;
- spelling and punctuation conventions;
- contractions or their absence;
- first/second-person habits;
- formal, technical, collegial, customer-facing, or conversational register;
- established terminology and capitalization.

Do not change `colour` to `color`, `organise` to `organize`, quotation style, date format, or vocabulary merely to make the document internally match a default locale.

## 2. Diagnose clusters, not words

The following are findings only when repeated, empty, structurally templated, or wrong for the voice and venue:

- `Moreover`, `Furthermore`, `Additionally`, `However`, `In conclusion`;
- `It is important to note`, `It is worth noting`, `In today's ...`;
- inflated abstractions such as `landscape`, `tapestry`, `paradigm`, `realm`;
- unsupported uplift such as `seamless`, `robust`, `transformative`, `powerful`;
- vague business verbs such as `leverage`, `enhance`, `optimize`, `facilitate`;
- repeated “not only X, but Y” or three-part rhetorical lists;
- every paragraph ending in a takeaway sentence.

Any of these can be correct. The defect is a cluster that carries little information or overrides the author's normal voice.

## 3. Prefer direct grammar when it improves the sentence

- Replace nominalizations with verbs when responsibility or action becomes clearer.
- Remove passive voice only when the actor matters and is known.
- Delete throat-clearing that delays the actual claim.
- Keep technical repetition when synonyms would reduce precision.
- Keep a formal sentence formal when the venue calls for it.

Do not add contractions, slang, jokes, fragments, idioms, or first-person claims merely to sound human.

## 4. Rhythm and structure

- Vary sentence and paragraph length only where meaning supports it.
- Preserve characteristic fragments, parentheticals, and interruptions.
- Do not force every paragraph into claim → evidence → summary.
- Do not convert every list into three items or every ending into a slogan.
- A plain sentence is allowed to remain plain.

## 5. Locale and voice preservation

Preserve:

- contractions when the author consistently uses them, and full forms when they do not;
- accepted regional vocabulary and institutional terms;
- degrees of certainty (`may`, `likely`, `will`) unless evidence changes;
- first-person stance and emotional temperature;
- deliberate repetition and understated humor;
- domain-specific capitalization and style guides.

## 6. Venue notes

### Technical and professional prose

Put the answer or impact first, use evidence per claim, and prefer concrete actors and conditions. Do not “humanize” logs, code, identifiers, or compliance language.

### Social prose

Keep the author's actual degree of informality. Do not auto-insert hooks, rhetorical questions, emoji, contractions, or punchline endings.

### Formal institutional prose

Formality is not an AI tell. Fix empty abstraction and indirectness while preserving required ceremony and legal precision.
